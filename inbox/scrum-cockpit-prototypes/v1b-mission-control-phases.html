1b · Mission Control, by phase the pane follows the sprint's shape

Same card language as variant 1, but the middle of the pane changes with the phase /sprint is in. Refinement runs every story at once, so the pane shows the stories still refining first, with their agent folded into the row, and counts for everything else. Build runs one story at a time, so that story becomes the focus card: its stage, its deliverables and the at most three agents working on it, while the rest of the sprint shrinks to a row of chips.

Phase, focus story and stage come from progress.md's trail lines (refine · … · started, 026 · D4 … · started, 026 · verify, 026 · review 1, gate); the agents from agent.spawn / tool.call, matched to a story by the id in their description.

Sprint phase:
Rules that keep it readable at any size
  • Refinement: stories still refining come first, longest-running on top; at most 8 rows, then +N more running. Refined stories collapse to id chips, and the header carries the count (3/12). Each agent is one story's refine, so the agents card shrinks to counts, model and the longest runner instead of repeating the list.
  • Quiet agent: no tool call for 3 minutes turns its row amber and puts its id in the agents summary. The sprint command's own rule, a trail silent for 15 minutes, turns the pulse amber with the time.
  • Build: exactly one focus card: the story, its stage (D1…Dn → Verify → Review → Done, with the current one outlined), when the step started, and the agents as a tree (builder → deliverable or review agent), never more than three rows. Story times so far give the estimate.
  • Sprint: one row of chips (✓ done, ● focus, ○ queued); a blocked story is an amber chip. Hovering a chip could open a card with its time and commit.
  • Gate and Review use the same focus card: the suite and the age of its last log line, or the review in progress.
